Every storybook has to start somewhere, and this one started with a question.

It was an ordinary Tuesday, and Rushu was supposed to be falling asleep. Instead, he was staring at the ceiling fan, watching it spin, when he asked: *"Mama, if I could ride on the fan, would I fly to the moon, or just get really, really dizzy?"*

His mother laughed, tucked the blanket up under his chin, and told him a story about a boy who rode a ceiling fan all the way past the clouds, past the birds, past the very last airplane, until he landed softly on a moon made of powdered sugar. Rushu fell asleep before the story even ended.

The next morning, she wrote it down. Just in case he asked again. Just in case she forgot the best parts.

That single page turned into a notebook. The notebook turned into a habit. And the habit turned into this: a small, cozy corner of the internet where Rushu's questions, worries, and wild ideas get to live on as stories.

Some of these tales are almost true. Some are completely made up. All of them started the same way &mdash; with a boy who wouldn't stop wondering, and a mother who couldn't stop listening.

More stories are on their way. Sleep tight.
